Understanding the Cocker Spaniel's coat

The Cocker Spaniel has a medium-length silky coat with long, flowing ear feathering and leg furnishings. The body coat lies flat and is relatively easy to maintain, but the ears, legs, and chest feathering are prone to matting — particularly in and around the ear flap, where the long ear hair traps moisture and can develop yeast and skin issues if not kept clean and groomed. Cocker Spaniels require a groomer who understands both the body clip and the feathering, and who pays careful attention to ear health. Understanding this coat structure is essential to grooming the breed correctly — and it is why breed-specific experience matters when choosing a groomer for your Cocker Spaniel.

What does a Cocker Spaniel groom include at Golden Paws?

A Cocker Spaniel groom at Golden Paws includes pre-bath brush-out, bath with coat and conditioner appropriate for the silky Cocker coat, blow-dry, breed-appropriate body clip, careful ear cleaning and ear feathering trim, leg and chest feathering trim, paw trim, nail trim, and anal gland expression. We pay particular attention to the ears — Cocker Spaniels are prone to ear infections and we check the ear canal at every appointment, cleaning thoroughly and noting any signs of redness, odor, or discharge. Every appointment is tailored to your individual dog's coat condition, skin health, and grooming history.

How often should a Cocker Spaniel be professionally groomed?

Cocker Spaniels benefit from professional grooming every 6 to 8 weeks. Cocker Spaniels with longer feathering should come in every 6 weeks to prevent matting in the ears and legs. The ear cleaning component is especially important for this breed — Cocker ears hang close to the head and restrict airflow, creating warm, moist conditions that encourage bacterial and yeast growth if not cleaned regularly. Our groomers assess the coat at every visit and will recommend any adjustments to the schedule based on your dog's individual coat condition and the time of year.
